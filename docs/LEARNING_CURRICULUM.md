# Learning curriculum

## 1. Curriculum model

Nadiano uses a spiral curriculum. Technique, reading, rhythm, ear training, theory, creativity, repertoire and practice skills develop in parallel and return at increasing difficulty.

Each stage has required outcomes rather than a fixed number of days. Learners may progress at different speeds in different competency areas. The recommendation engine should assign review work where one area falls behind.

## 2. Competency areas

Every lesson and exercise is tagged with one or more competencies:

| Code | Competency | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| `body` | setup and healthy movement | seat, shoulders, wrist, release |
| `technique` | keyboard movement | transfer, thumb passage, chords, leaps |
| `rhythm` | pulse and duration | counting, rests, subdivisions, syncopation |
| `reading` | notation reading | landmarks, intervals, voices, score navigation |
| `ear` | listening | direction, imitation, intervals, chords, dictation |
| `theory` | musical understanding | keys, scales, harmony, form |
| `expression` | musical control | articulation, dynamics, phrasing, voicing, pedal |
| `creative` | creation and application | improvisation, transposition, accompaniment |
| `repertoire` | complete music | preparation, interpretation, continuity |
| `practice` | learning process | diagnosis, looping, tempo planning, memory |
| `performance` | reliable delivery | run-throughs, recovery, recording, stage routine |

## 3. Foundation stage F0 — instrument and body

### Outcomes

The learner can identify the keyboard layout, name finger numbers, prepare a stable seated position and play isolated tones without obvious unnecessary force.

### Lessons

1. **Meet the keyboard**
   - groups of two and three black keys;
   - high, low, left and right;
   - find repeated pitch classes without note names.
2. **Sitting and distance**
   - sit near the front half of the seat;
   - elbows approximately level with the keyboard;
   - feet supported;
   - enough distance for free elbow movement.
3. **Release unnecessary tension**
   - check jaw, shoulders and hands;
   - contrast rigid and released movement;
   - stop immediately for pain.
4. **Finger numbers**
   - 1–5 in both hands;
   - tap named fingers away from the keyboard;
   - avoid tying one finger permanently to one note.
5. **Natural hand shape**
   - relaxed curve, visible knuckles, supported fingertip;
   - thumb contacts from the side;
   - no forced “ball” shape.
6. **Arm support and release**
   - allow supported arm weight into one tone;
   - release after the tone;
   - distinguish weight from pushing.
7. **First controlled tones**
   - play and release one black key with fingers 2–4;
   - listen for an even, unforced result.

### App presentation

- short original top and side animations;
- correct/incorrect comparison;
- dry exercise before MIDI exercise;
- one cue per attempt;
- self-check after the measurable task.

### Alpha content target

All seven lessons exist in the alpha, using simple original exercises.

## 4. Foundation stage F1 — pulse, sound and movement

### Outcomes

The learner follows a steady beat, distinguishes basic sound qualities and plays short one-hand patterns with controlled release.

### Topics

- steady pulse and count-in;
- quarter, half and whole durations;
- rests as active counted time;
- repeated, ascending and descending tones;
- loud/soft as controlled ranges, not force;
- legato and detached touch;
- phrase ending and wrist release;
- echoing two- and three-note patterns;
- alternating hands;
- simple call and response improvisation.

### Technique sequence

1. supported single tone;
2. two-finger transfer;
3. three-finger transfer;
4. repeated notes with release;
5. short connected phrase;
6. detached movement using arm and finger together.

### Reading approach

Use contour and rhythm before dense staff reading. The learner should understand that notation represents sound and time, not only button positions.

## 5. Beginner stage B1 — landmarks and intervals

### Outcomes

The learner reads elementary material in treble and bass clefs using landmarks, direction and intervals rather than counting every line and space.

### Topics

- five-line staff, clefs and grand staff;
- middle C, treble G and bass F as landmarks;
- repeated note, step and skip;
- seconds through fifths;
- measures and bar lines;
- 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4;
- quarter, half, dotted half and whole rests;
- hands separately in varied positions;
- look-read-prepare-play routine;
- short sight-reading cards.

### Rules

- Do not teach a permanent “middle C position”.
- Use multiple keyboard areas early.
- Ask the learner to say direction and interval before note names where useful.
- Include bass clef from the beginning of staff reading.

## 6. Beginner stage B2 — five-finger patterns and coordination

### Outcomes

The learner plays simple two-hand textures, reads common five-note patterns and can transpose a pattern by shape and sound.

### Topics

- major and minor five-finger patterns;
- C, G, D, F and related minor areas;
- hands alternating and together;
- similar and contrary motion;
- melody with held tone or simple blocked accompaniment;
- eighth notes and basic subdivision;
- dotted quarter plus eighth;
- pickup measures;
- phrase marks and breathing points;
- simple tonic and dominant sounds;
- transposition of short patterns;
- improvisation using limited note sets.

### Technique

- transfer weight without collapsing joints;
- maintain a free thumb;
- small lateral wrist adjustment;
- prepare position changes before playing;
- play simple blocked chords as one coordinated gesture.

## 7. Elementary stage E1 — scales, chords and pedal

### Outcomes

The learner understands keys and basic harmony, plays introductory scales and chords with planned fingering, and uses sustain pedal in short controlled contexts.

### Topics

- major scales introduced through tetrachords;
- one-octave scales hands separately, then selected hands together;
- natural, harmonic and melodic minor concepts;
- thumb passage as coordinated lateral movement;
- major and minor triads;
- inversions;
- tonic, subdominant and dominant functions;
- I–IV–V–I and related cadences;
- broken chord patterns;
- introductory arpeggios;
- syncopation and sixteenth-note preparation;
- pedal change after harmony changes;
- melody/accompaniment balance;
- chord symbols and simple lead sheets.

### Pedal teaching sequence

1. listen to dry and sustained sound;
2. press after the played harmony;
3. release and replace at a change;
4. compare clean and blurred versions;
5. apply to a short phrase;
6. self-assess because basic MIDI pedal events do not prove acoustic cleanliness.

## 8. Intermediate stage I1 — independence and texture

### Outcomes

The learner controls different roles between hands and voices and can diagnose common coordination problems.

### Topics

- melody against repeated accompaniment;
- Alberti and waltz patterns;
- two-voice counterpoint;
- voicing within chords;
- larger position shifts and leaps;
- repeated-note patterns;
- ornaments;
- compound meter;
- syncopation;
- phrase shaping and basic rubato;
- harmonic and formal analysis;
- sight-reading with simplification strategies;
- melody and bass reproduction by ear;
- improvisation over tonic, predominant and dominant functions.

### Practice skills

- isolate a voice;
- block a broken texture;
- reduce rhythm or notes temporarily;
- practise landing points for leaps;
- use silent preparation;
- compare planned and actual tempo;
- record one complete run and annotate problems.

## 9. Intermediate stage I2 — fluency and style

### Outcomes

The learner handles longer pieces and contrasting styles with reliable technique and an intentional practice plan.

### Topics

- scales and arpeggios in more keys;
- chromatic scale;
- double-note preparation;
- octave preparation without repetitive strain;
- faster chord changes;
- layered pedalling;
- Baroque articulation and voicing;
- Classical balance and clarity;
- Romantic phrase and rubato control;
- introductory Impressionist sound and pedal;
- modern meters and rhythmic patterns;
- accompaniment from chord symbols;
- ensemble preparation with a reference track.

## 10. Advanced stage A1 — comprehensive technique and musicianship

### Outcomes

The learner can plan fingering, technique and interpretation for substantial repertoire and maintain broad musicianship skills.

### Topics

- scales, contrary motion and arpeggios in all keys;
- selected thirds, sixths, chords and octaves;
- repeated notes and fast position changes;
- advanced pedalling and half-pedal concepts;
- multi-voice balance and contrapuntal clarity;
- historical style and source awareness;
- complex rhythm, tuplets and cross-rhythm;
- advanced sight-reading and score reduction;
- chord progression, melodic and rhythmic dictation;
- modulation and formal analysis;
- improvisation in defined styles;
- accompaniment and chamber-music awareness.

The app supports planning and measurable repetition but requires teacher oversight for demanding physical techniques.

## 11. Pre-professional stage P1 — preparation skills

### Outcomes

The learner can prepare a varied programme, evaluate recordings, recover from mistakes and explain interpretive decisions.

### Topics

- programme building across periods;
- edition and source comparison;
- personal fingering design and testing;
- memory through structure, harmony, hearing and motor cues;
- performance run-through schedules;
- recovery points and continuation after mistakes;
- stage routine and concentration;
- recording review with written criteria;
- advanced sight-reading and accompaniment;
- audition or examination preparation;
- collaboration with a qualified teacher.

Nadiano must not mark this stage as independently certifying professional competence.

## 12. Lesson template

Every guided lesson contains:

1. **Prerequisites** — skills that must already be introduced.
2. **Learning goal** — observable learner outcome.
3. **Why it matters** — one brief explanation.
4. **Demonstration** — original animation, score and/or audio.
5. **Common mistake** — specific contrast without fear-based wording.
6. **Dry task** — optional movement or counting away from the keys.
7. **Guided task** — low-complexity application.
8. **Musical task** — phrase or mini-piece.
9. **Feedback mapping** — what MIDI categories are assessed.
10. **Self-check** — no more than three non-MIDI questions.
11. **Completion rule** — explicit evidence needed.
12. **Review schedule** — when the skill returns.

## 13. Session template

A default 20-minute beginner session:

- 2 minutes: setup and physical cue;
- 4 minutes: technique;
- 4 minutes: rhythm or reading;
- 3 minutes: ear training;
- 6 minutes: repertoire;
- 1 minute: reflection and next task.

Shorter and longer sessions use the same balance rather than removing one competency permanently.

## 14. Progression rules

- New tempo increases only after at least two successful controlled repetitions.
- Repeated pitch errors trigger a reduced note set or hands-separate task.
- Repeated timing errors trigger clapping, counting or rhythm-only practice.
- A physical technique cue never receives an automatic “correct” judgment from MIDI.
- Sight-reading attempts are limited and use unfamiliar generated or curated material.
- Review scheduling considers last result, error type, number of attempts and time since practice.
- Completion is competency-based; an arbitrary daily streak is not a substitute.

## 15. Content quantity targets

### Alpha

- 7 F0 lessons;
- 5 F1 lessons;
- at least 20 short exercises;
- 4 ear tasks;
- 3 original mini-pieces.

### Beta

- complete F0, F1, B1 and B2 path;
- at least 45 guided lessons;
- at least 100 exercises;
- at least 30 ear tasks;
- at least 15 original mini-pieces;
- generated reading and rhythm cards.

### 1.0

- 60 or more guided lessons through introductory E1 material;
- 120 or more technique/rhythm exercises;
- 80 or more reading configurations;
- 60 or more ear tasks;
- 24 original mini-pieces;
- 12 verified public-domain melodies in Nadiano's own edition;
- stage checks and a final beginner assessment.

## 16. Content review requirements

Before release, every learning item is reviewed for:

- musical correctness;
- technical safety and honest measurement claims;
- age-neutral and culturally neutral language;
- German and Indonesian equivalence;
- fingering practicality on common 61- and 88-key instruments;
- accessible visual and audio presentation;
- licensing and source status;
- deterministic expected-event generation.
